# Card patterns by meeting type

Starting points, not templates to fill blindly. Pick the closest one, drop the cards that don't apply, and keep the user's own content and wording.

## Contents

- Sales / client call
- Presentation or talk
- Recorded video / speech (teleprompter)
- Job interview (as a candidate)
- Interviewing someone (as the interviewer)
- 1:1 with a manager or report
- Negotiation
- Status update / standup
- Class, workshop or training
- Podcast or panel guest
- Backup card bank (any meeting)

## Sales / client call

Opening (thanks, goal, agenda) → Discovery questions → Their pain in their words (filled live, or known facts) → Demo flow as a checklist → Proof (one case study, one number) → Pricing (exact numbers, what's negotiable) → Close (the ask, verbatim) → Backup: objections, competitor comparisons.

Tips: put the **ask** in a quote (`> "..."`) so it's said deliberately; highlight the one number they must quote correctly.

## Presentation or talk

Hybrid format works best: a scripted hook (60 to 90 words) → one cue card per slide or section, titled like the slide so the user can keep them in sync → a scripted closing → Backup: likely questions with 1-line answers.

Tips: add minute markers to titles if there's a time limit (`## Results (min 8)`); put transitions as the last bullet of each card ("→ next: the pricing model").

## Recorded video / speech (teleprompter)

Full script, one short paragraph per card (60 to 90 words), written for the ear: short sentences, contractions, no parentheses. Put **bold** on the words to stress. Optional stage cues in italics on their own line (`*pause*`, `*smile, slower*`). Start with a `##` heading per card that names the beat ("Hook", "Story", "The turn", "Call to action") so the next-card preview helps pacing.

## Job interview (as a candidate)

Cards: My 60-second pitch (scripted) → Why this company / role (3 bullets with specifics) → STAR stories, one per card (`## Story: led the migration`; Situation · Task · Action · **Result with number**) → Answers to likely tough questions (gaps, salary, weaknesses) → Questions to ask them → Logistics (names of interviewers, salary range, notice period).

Keep stories as keywords, not scripts. A read-aloud answer sounds rehearsed.

## Interviewing someone (as the interviewer)

Cards: Welcome & format (time, structure, they can ask questions at the end) → one card per competency with 2 or 3 questions and follow-ups → What a strong answer sounds like (for calibration) → Their questions / selling points about the role → Close and next steps (timeline, who contacts them).

## 1:1 with a manager or report

Cards: Wins since last time → Topics, the hard one first, framed as a question → The ask (verbatim if sensitive, e.g. promotion, raise, change of team) with supporting evidence (3 bullets, numbers) → Feedback to give or request → Agreed actions as `- [ ]`.

## Negotiation

Cards: Goal / walk-away / ideal (==the walk-away number==) → Their likely priorities → Opening position (verbatim) → Concessions ladder in order, each with what to get in return → Questions to ask before conceding → Backup: their likely objections with responses → Close: summarize, confirm in writing.

## Status update / standup

One card per project or stream: status in 1 line (✓ on track / ⚠ at risk / ✗ blocked) → what's done → what's next → **what I need from whom**. Keep each to about 4 bullets.

## Class, workshop or training

Card per segment with time markers: objective → explanation keywords → example or exercise instructions (numbered steps) → check question for the group. Add a materials checklist card at the start and a homework / recap card at the end.

## Podcast or panel guest

Cards: 30-second bio (scripted) → 3 to 5 key messages, one card each, with a story or number → Answers to expected questions → Things to plug (book, link said aloud, handle) → Topics to avoid or redirect.

## Backup card bank (any meeting)

Optional cards at the end, titled `## Backup: ...`: objections, tough questions, key numbers, names and roles of attendees, a glossary of their terms. The user jumps there with `Ctrl+Alt+End` (last card) or by pressing `Ctrl+Alt+→` a few times, so keep backup cards together at the end and say so in the delivery summary.
